Oncology

Bladder Cancer

The most common urologic cancer in both men and women. Early detection through evaluation of blood in the urine leads to the best outcomes.

Home›Conditions›Bladder Cancer

What Is Bladder Cancer?

Bladder cancer begins in the cells lining the inside of the bladder — a hollow organ in the pelvis that stores urine. The most common type is urothelial carcinoma (also called transitional cell carcinoma), accounting for about 90% of cases. It is the fourth most common cancer in men and affects women as well.

Bladder cancer is classified as non-muscle-invasive (confined to the bladder lining) or muscle-invasive (spread into bladder muscle). Non-muscle-invasive disease is treatable with endoscopic surgery and intravesical therapy, but requires lifelong surveillance due to its tendency to recur.

Common Symptoms

Blood in urine (hematuria) — often painless and the most common early sign
Urinary urgency or frequency without infection
Painful urination (dysuria)
Pelvic pain or lower back pain
Recurrent urinary tract infections
Inability to urinate (urinary retention) in advanced cases
Unexplained weight loss and fatigue (advanced disease)
Leg swelling due to lymph node involvement

Causes & Risk Factors

Tobacco Smoking

The single greatest risk factor; smokers are 2–3× more likely to develop bladder cancer.

Chemical Exposure

Occupational exposure to aromatic amines (dye, rubber, leather industries).

Age & Sex

Risk increases with age; men are 3–4× more likely than women to develop it.

Chronic Bladder Irritation

Recurrent infections, bladder stones, or indwelling catheters may raise risk.

Prior Radiation or Chemotherapy

Cyclophosphamide and pelvic radiation increase long-term bladder cancer risk.

Family History

A family history of bladder or Lynch syndrome–related cancers increases risk.

Treatment Options

Minimally Invasive

TURBT (Transurethral Resection)

  • Removal of tumors through a cystoscope — no incisions
  • First-line treatment for non-muscle-invasive disease
  • Blue-light cystoscopy to detect flat tumors
  • Performed under anesthesia as outpatient procedure
Intravesical

Bladder Instillation Therapy

  • BCG (Bacillus Calmette-Guérin) immunotherapy — gold standard
  • Intravesical chemotherapy (gemcitabine, mitomycin)
  • Reduces recurrence and progression risk
  • Weekly instillations then maintenance schedule
Surgical

Radical Cystectomy

  • Removal of the bladder for muscle-invasive disease
  • Robotic-assisted approach when appropriate
  • Urinary diversion: ileal conduit or neobladder
  • Combined with neoadjuvant chemotherapy
Surveillance

Ongoing Monitoring

  • Regular cystoscopy every 3–6 months initially
  • Urine cytology and biomarker testing
  • CT urography to evaluate upper tracts
  • Lifelong surveillance required for all stages

When to See Dr. Hamoui — Don't Wait

  • Any blood in urine — even once — warrants prompt evaluation
  • Urinary urgency or frequency that doesn't respond to antibiotic treatment
  • You smoke and have new urinary symptoms
  • You have a history of bladder cancer and are due for surveillance
  • Unexplained pelvic pain or recurrent UTIs in an adult
