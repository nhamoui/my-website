Kidney Cancer

Renal cell carcinoma is often found incidentally on imaging. When treated early with nephron-sparing surgery, the prognosis is excellent.

What Is Kidney Cancer?

Kidney cancer (renal cell carcinoma, or RCC) originates in the cells of the kidney tubules. It is the seventh most common cancer in the United States. Most kidney tumors are discovered incidentally — found on imaging ordered for an unrelated reason — before symptoms develop. This "incidental" detection often means the cancer is caught at an early, highly curable stage.

Dr. Hamoui specializes in nephron-sparing (kidney-preserving) surgery, performing robotic partial nephrectomy to remove tumors while saving as much healthy kidney tissue as possible — protecting long-term kidney function.

Common Symptoms

Often no symptoms in early stage (discovered incidentally on imaging)
Blood in urine (hematuria) — painless
Persistent flank or back pain on one side
Palpable mass in the abdomen or flank
Unexplained fatigue and weight loss
Intermittent fever without infection
Anemia (low red blood cell count)
High blood pressure that is difficult to control

Causes & Risk Factors

Tobacco Use

Smokers have approximately twice the risk of developing kidney cancer.

Obesity

Excess body weight is one of the strongest modifiable risk factors.

High Blood Pressure

Hypertension is independently associated with increased kidney cancer risk.

Genetic Conditions

Von Hippel-Lindau syndrome, hereditary papillary RCC, and Birt-Hogg-Dubé syndrome markedly elevate risk.

Family History

First-degree relatives of kidney cancer patients have a 2-4× increased risk.

Chronic Kidney Disease

Long-term dialysis is associated with acquired cystic kidney disease and renal tumors.

Treatment Options

Surgical

Robotic Partial Nephrectomy

  • Removes tumor while preserving healthy kidney tissue
  • Preferred for tumors ≤7 cm (Stage I–II)
  • da Vinci robotic system enables precise dissection
  • Protects long-term kidney function better than radical nephrectomy
Surgical

Robotic Radical Nephrectomy

  • Complete removal of the kidney for large or centrally located tumors
  • Minimally invasive approach with 3–4 small incisions
  • Lymph node dissection when indicated
  • May include adrenal gland removal for upper pole tumors
Ablation

Thermal Ablation

  • Cryoablation or radiofrequency ablation (RFA)
  • Image-guided destruction of small tumors (<3 cm)
  • Ideal for patients who cannot undergo surgery
  • Can be repeated if necessary
Systemic / Surveillance

Active Surveillance & Medical Therapy

  • Observation for small (<2 cm), slow-growing masses in elderly or frail patients
  • Targeted therapy (sunitinib, pazopanib) for metastatic disease
  • Immunotherapy (nivolumab + ipilimumab) combinations
  • Coordinated with medical oncology for advanced stage

When to See Dr. Hamoui

  • A kidney mass or lesion was found incidentally on CT, MRI, or ultrasound
  • You have blood in your urine that your primary care doctor cannot explain
  • You have a family history of kidney cancer or a genetic syndrome (VHL, BHD)
  • You experience unexplained flank pain, weight loss, or persistent fatigue
  • You have been diagnosed with kidney cancer and are seeking a surgical opinion
