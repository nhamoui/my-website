Men's Health

Prostatitis

Inflammation of the prostate can cause significant pain and urinary symptoms — accurate diagnosis is key to effective treatment.

Home › Conditions › Prostatitis

What Is Prostatitis?

Prostatitis is inflammation of the prostate gland. Unlike BPH or prostate cancer, prostatitis can affect men of any age and is the most common urologic diagnosis in men under 50. It accounts for approximately 2 million outpatient visits per year in the United States.

There are four recognized categories of prostatitis, each with different causes and treatments:

  • Category I — Acute bacterial prostatitis: A sudden bacterial infection requiring prompt antibiotic treatment
  • Category II — Chronic bacterial prostatitis: Recurrent urinary tract infections traced to the prostate as a reservoir
  • Category III — Chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome (CP/CPPS): The most common form; pelvic pain lasting 3+ months without a confirmed infection
  • Category IV — Asymptomatic inflammatory prostatitis: Found incidentally; no treatment typically required

Common Symptoms

  • Pelvic, perineal, or rectal pain or pressure
  • Pain or burning with urination
  • Urinary urgency and frequency
  • Weak or interrupted urine stream
  • Pain during or after ejaculation
  • Genital discomfort (scrotal, penile, or inner thigh pain)
  • Fever and chills (in acute bacterial prostatitis)
  • Lower back pain

Acute bacterial prostatitis may present with sudden high fever, severe pelvic pain, and difficulty urinating. Chronic forms tend to have waxing and waning symptoms over months to years.

Causes & Risk Factors

Bacterial prostatitis is caused by pathogens — typically gram-negative organisms such as E. coli — that infect the prostate via the urethra or bloodstream. The cause of CP/CPPS is poorly understood but likely involves a combination of:

  • Pelvic floor muscle tension and dysfunction
  • Nerve sensitization and central pain amplification
  • Immune and inflammatory dysregulation
  • Prior urinary tract or sexually transmitted infections
  • Stress and psychological factors

Risk factors include:

  • Prior episode of prostatitis
  • Recent urinary catheterization or cystoscopy
  • Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)
  • High levels of psychological stress
  • Prolonged sitting (cyclists, office workers)

Treatment Options

Antibiotic Therapy

Acute and chronic bacterial prostatitis are treated with antibiotics — typically fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin) or trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for 4–6 weeks. Acute bacterial prostatitis may require IV antibiotics initially. Culture-guided selection is used when possible to address resistance.

Alpha-Blockers & Anti-Inflammatories

Alpha-blockers (tamsulosin, alfuzosin) relax smooth muscle in the prostate and bladder neck, improving urinary flow and reducing pain in many men with CP/CPPS. NSAIDs provide symptomatic pain relief. 5-alpha reductase inhibitors may be added for men with concurrent BPH.

Pelvic Floor Physical Therapy & Neuromodulation

Many men with CP/CPPS have significant pelvic floor hypertonicity. Specialized pelvic floor physical therapy — including myofascial release and trigger point therapy — can provide substantial relief. Neuromodulation techniques (sacral neuromodulation) may help refractory cases with bladder-related symptoms.

Procedural & Multimodal Approaches

Transurethral microwave thermotherapy (TUMT) and minimally invasive procedures may help selected patients. For refractory CP/CPPS, a multimodal approach combining medication, physical therapy, and psychological support yields the best long-term outcomes. Dr. Hamoui coordinates comprehensive care tailored to each patient's specific symptom profile.

When to See a Doctor

  • Seek same-day care for fever, chills, and severe difficulty urinating — this may be acute bacterial prostatitis or a prostatic abscess
  • Pelvic or perineal pain lasting more than a few weeks
  • Recurrent urinary tract infections
  • Pain with ejaculation or significant sexual dysfunction
  • Symptoms that are not improving despite prior antibiotic courses

Get Accurate Diagnosis & Relief

Prostatitis is frequently misdiagnosed. Dr. Hamoui provides expert evaluation to identify the specific type and target treatment accordingly.
