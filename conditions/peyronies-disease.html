Men's Health

Peyronie's Disease

Penile curvature caused by scar tissue is a real medical condition — not something men simply have to live with. Effective treatments are available.

Home › Conditions › Peyronie's Disease

What Is Peyronie's Disease?

Peyronie's disease is a connective tissue disorder in which fibrous scar tissue (plaque) forms within the tunica albuginea — the thick sheath of tissue surrounding the erectile chambers of the penis. This plaque causes the penis to curve, bend, or indent during erections, often causing pain and difficulty with sexual intercourse.

Peyronie's disease affects approximately 1 in 11 men between the ages of 40 and 70, though it can occur at any age. Many men feel embarrassed to seek care, yet the condition is well-recognized in urology and has multiple effective treatment options — particularly when addressed during the active (acute) phase.

Common Symptoms

  • Penile curvature (upward, downward, or lateral) during erections
  • Pain during erections or intercourse
  • A palpable lump or hard area along the shaft of the penis
  • Penile shortening or narrowing ("bottleneck" or "hourglass" deformity)
  • Difficulty achieving or maintaining an erection (associated ED)
  • Anxiety and distress related to sexual performance

Peyronie's disease has two phases. In the acute phase (first 6–18 months), the plaque is forming — curvature may worsen and pain is common. In the chronic/stable phase, the plaque has stabilized and pain typically resolves, but deformity persists.

Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of Peyronie's disease is not fully known, but it is believed to result from repetitive microtrauma to the penis during sexual activity, triggering an abnormal wound-healing response in susceptible individuals.

  • Prior penile injury: Bending or buckling forces during intercourse
  • Genetic predisposition: Family history of Peyronie's or Dupuytren's contracture (a related condition affecting the hand)
  • Connective tissue disorders: Men with Dupuytren's, Ledderhose disease, or similar conditions have higher rates
  • Age: Most common in men 40–70
  • Diabetes: Associated with higher risk and more severe disease
  • Radical prostatectomy: Surgical trauma can trigger Peyronie's in a subset of men

Treatment Options

Treatment is selected based on severity, phase of disease, degree of curvature, presence of ED, and patient goals. Dr. Hamoui provides individualized counseling on all options.

Observation (Stable, Mild Disease)

Men with mild curvature (<30°) that does not interfere with intercourse may choose watchful waiting. Approximately 10–15% of cases improve spontaneously. Oral vitamin E (historically used) has limited evidence; Dr. Hamoui counsels patients on current evidence-based data for all supplements and medications.

Intralesional Collagenase (Xiaflex)

Collagenase clostridium histolyticum (Xiaflex) is the only FDA-approved non-surgical treatment for Peyronie's disease. Injected directly into the plaque in office-based cycles, Xiaflex enzymatically breaks down the collagen fibers, reducing curvature. Best results are seen in the acute phase with dorsal or lateral curvature of ≥30°. Multiple injection cycles are typically required.

Verapamil & Interferon Injections

Off-label intralesional verapamil or interferon alpha-2b may be used when Xiaflex is not appropriate or available. These agents can stabilize plaque and reduce pain during the acute phase. They are less well-studied than Xiaflex but provide an alternative for select patients.

Surgical Correction

Surgery is indicated for stable disease (no change for ≥3 months) with curvature that prevents intercourse. Options include: plication (shortening the longer side — suitable for mild–moderate curvature without significant shortening), plaque incision/grafting (corrects severe curvature with some length restoration), and penile prosthesis implantation (definitive treatment when ED coexists — straightening occurs during implantation). Dr. Hamoui evaluates each patient's anatomy and ED status to recommend the optimal surgical approach.

When to See a Doctor

  • You notice a new bend, curve, or deformity in your erections
  • You feel a hard lump or area of thickening on the penis
  • Pain is occurring with erections or intercourse
  • Curvature is making intercourse difficult or impossible
  • You are experiencing associated erectile dysfunction
  • Symptoms are causing psychological distress or relationship strain

Early evaluation during the acute phase opens the most treatment options, including Xiaflex. Don't wait.
