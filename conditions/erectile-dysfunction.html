Men's Health

Erectile Dysfunction

Difficulty achieving or maintaining an erection is more common than most men realize — and highly treatable with the right care.

Home › Conditions › Erectile Dysfunction

What Is Erectile Dysfunction?

Erectile dysfunction (ED) is the persistent inability to achieve or maintain an erection firm enough for satisfactory sexual activity. While occasional difficulty is normal, ED that occurs regularly warrants medical evaluation. ED affects an estimated 30 million men in the United States and becomes more common with age — though it is not an inevitable part of aging.

ED is often a sign of an underlying medical or psychological condition. Because the process of achieving an erection requires coordinated input from the nervous system, blood vessels, hormones, and psychology, any disruption along this pathway can result in ED. The good news: with proper diagnosis, the vast majority of men can be successfully treated.

Common Symptoms

  • Trouble getting an erection
  • Difficulty maintaining an erection during sexual activity
  • Reduced sexual desire or libido
  • Erections that are not firm enough for penetration
  • Anxiety or stress specifically related to sexual performance
  • Relationship strain due to sexual difficulties

ED may present gradually (worsening over months) or suddenly. Sudden onset often suggests a psychological component, while gradual onset is more typically associated with vascular or hormonal causes.

Causes & Risk Factors

ED is most often caused by reduced blood flow to the penis, nerve damage, hormonal imbalance, or psychological factors — frequently a combination of these.

Physical causes include:

  • Cardiovascular disease and atherosclerosis (hardening of the arteries)
  • Diabetes mellitus (nerve and vascular damage)
  • High blood pressure and high cholesterol
  • Obesity and metabolic syndrome
  • Low testosterone (hypogonadism)
  • Peyronie's disease (penile scar tissue)
  • Neurological conditions (Parkinson's, multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injury)
  • Side effects of medications (antidepressants, antihypertensives, antiandrogens)
  • Prior pelvic surgery or radiation (e.g., radical prostatectomy)
  • Sleep disorders

Psychological causes include:

  • Stress, anxiety, and depression
  • Performance anxiety
  • Relationship problems
  • Past sexual trauma
ED as a cardiovascular warning sign: Because the penile arteries are smaller than coronary arteries, ED may precede cardiovascular disease by 3–5 years. New-onset ED warrants a cardiovascular risk assessment in addition to urologic evaluation.

Treatment Options

Treatment is tailored to the underlying cause. Dr. Hamoui takes a thorough, individualized approach — beginning with the least invasive options and escalating when needed.

Oral Medications (PDE5 Inhibitors)

Sildenafil (Viagra), tadalafil (Cialis), and vardenafil (Levitra) are first-line therapy for most men with ED. They work by enhancing blood flow to the penis in response to sexual stimulation. Effective in up to 70% of men, they are convenient and well-tolerated for most patients.

Hormone Therapy

For men with confirmed low testosterone, testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) can improve libido and erectile function. TRT is available in several forms including topical gels, injections, and pellet implants. Dr. Hamoui coordinates hormone management alongside ED treatment when indicated.

Vacuum Erection Devices & Injection Therapy

For men who do not respond to oral medications, vacuum erection devices (VEDs) or penile self-injection therapy (alprostadil, trimix) are highly effective second-line options. Intraurethral alprostadil (MUSE) is another non-injection option. These approaches work regardless of the underlying vascular mechanism.

Penile Prosthesis (Implant)

For men who have failed or cannot use medical therapy, a penile prosthesis (inflatable or malleable implant) offers a permanent, highly satisfying solution. Patient satisfaction rates exceed 90%. Dr. Hamoui performs this procedure with minimally invasive techniques, ensuring optimal cosmetic and functional outcomes. This is the definitive surgical treatment for ED.

When to See a Doctor

  • ED is occurring regularly (not just occasionally)
  • ED is causing stress, anxiety, or relationship problems
  • You have diabetes, heart disease, or other conditions linked to ED
  • ED developed after pelvic surgery or radiation
  • You have other symptoms such as reduced libido or fatigue suggesting low testosterone
  • You have not had success with over-the-counter supplements (which are largely unregulated)

ED is a medical condition with effective, evidence-based treatments. There is no reason to accept it without evaluation.
