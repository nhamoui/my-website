Prostate Cancer

The most common cancer in American men. When caught early, prostate cancer is highly treatable with excellent long-term outcomes.

Home›Conditions›Prostate Cancer

What Is Prostate Cancer?

Prostate cancer is the uncontrolled growth of cells in the prostate gland — a walnut-sized gland that produces seminal fluid in men. It is the most common cancer in American men and the second leading cause of cancer death, though the vast majority of cases are slow-growing and highly treatable when detected early.

The disease ranges widely in behavior. Some prostate cancers grow slowly and may never cause symptoms or require treatment. Others are aggressive and can spread quickly. This makes personalized risk assessment — not a one-size-fits-all approach — essential to proper management.

Common Symptoms

Early prostate cancer often causes no symptoms. As the cancer grows, the following may develop:

Frequent urination, especially at night (nocturia)
Weak or interrupted urine stream
Difficulty starting or stopping urination
Blood in urine (hematuria) or semen (hematospermia)
Painful or burning urination
Erectile dysfunction
Pelvic discomfort or pressure
Bone pain (hips, back) — sign of advanced disease

Causes & Risk Factors

Age

Risk increases significantly after age 50; most cases are diagnosed after 65.

Family History

A father or brother with prostate cancer more than doubles your risk.

Race

African American men are at higher risk and are more likely to develop aggressive disease.

BRCA Gene Mutations

BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations increase risk of aggressive prostate cancer.

Diet & Lifestyle

High-fat diets and obesity may contribute to aggressive prostate cancer risk.

Elevated PSA

A rising PSA level over time may indicate cancer, though other conditions also raise PSA.

Treatment Options

Conservative

Active Surveillance

  • Appropriate for low-risk, slow-growing cancers
  • Regular PSA testing and prostate biopsies
  • MRI monitoring for tumor changes
  • Delays or avoids treatment side effects
Surgical

Robotic Prostatectomy (RALP)

  • Complete removal of the prostate gland
  • Performed with da Vinci robotic system
  • Nerve-sparing technique preserves function
  • Shorter hospital stay, faster recovery than open surgery
Radiation

Radiation Therapy

  • External beam radiation therapy (EBRT / IMRT)
  • Brachytherapy (radioactive seed implants)
  • Stereotactic body radiation (SBRT / CyberKnife)
  • Coordinated with radiation oncology team
Systemic

Hormone & Systemic Therapy

  • Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) for advanced disease
  • Abiraterone, enzalutamide for castrate-resistant cancer
  • Chemotherapy (docetaxel) for metastatic disease
  • Immunotherapy and PARP inhibitor options

When to See Dr. Hamoui

  • You are age 50 or older and have never had a PSA screening discussion
  • You are age 40–45 with a family history of prostate cancer or are African American
  • Your PSA level is elevated or rising on routine labs
  • You notice blood in your urine or semen
  • You have been diagnosed with prostate cancer and need surgical evaluation
  • You are on active surveillance and want a second opinion
